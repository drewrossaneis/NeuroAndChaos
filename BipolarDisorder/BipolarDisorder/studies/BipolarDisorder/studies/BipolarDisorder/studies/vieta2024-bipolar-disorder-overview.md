# Bipolar Disorder — Clinical Overview

## What is it?

Bipolar disorder is a chronic psychiatric condition marked by recurring episodes 
of mania and depression. It affects over 1% of the global population and is one 
of the leading causes of disability worldwide — not just from the episodes 
themselves, but from associated risks like suicide and cardiovascular disease.

## Types of episodes

- **Mania** — elevated or irritable mood, reduced need for sleep, impulsivity, 
  grandiosity, sometimes psychosis
- **Hypomania** — similar to mania but less severe, no psychosis
- **Depression** — low energy, hopelessness, cognitive impairment, suicidal ideation
- **Mixed states** — features of both mania and depression simultaneously

## Why is it hard to diagnose?

Symptoms overlap heavily with unipolar depression. Most patients are misdiagnosed 
for years before receiving a correct diagnosis. There are no specific biomarkers — 
diagnosis is entirely clinical.

## What causes it?

A combination of genetic, neurobiological, and environmental factors. No single 
cause has been identified. Comorbidities — including ADHD, anxiety disorders, 
and substance use — are common and complicate both diagnosis and treatment.

## Treatment

Lifelong medication is typically required, often more than one drug. 
Mood stabilizers (lithium, valproate), antipsychotics, and antidepressants 
are used in different combinations depending on the episode type and severity. 
Psychotherapy plays a supporting role but does not replace medication.

## Current research gaps

- No validated biomarkers for diagnosis
- Treatment response varies significantly between patients
- Access to specialized care remains unequal globally
- Precision psychiatry and digital health tools are emerging but not yet standard
